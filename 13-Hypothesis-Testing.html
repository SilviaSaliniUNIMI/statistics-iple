What is hypothesis testing?

It is a procedure that uses sample data to assess whether a statement about the population is compatible with what we have observed.

The logic of hypothesis testing
1Formulate a question about the population
2Define H0 and H1
3Observe the sampling outcome
4Assess how plausible it is under H0
Null hypothesis H0

It is the reference statement that we provisionally assume to be true in order to construct the test: no change, no effect, or a specific parameter value.

Alternative hypothesis H1

It describes the change or effect we want to detect: a value that is different from, greater than, or less than the one specified by H0.

Sampling as an experiment. More precisely, drawing the sample is the random experiment; the observed sample is its outcome. The statistic calculated from the data is the result we compare with what H0 would make plausible.
Analogy with proof by contradiction. We assume H0 and examine its consequences. If the observed result would be very rare under H0, we reject it. The analogy is not perfect: we do not obtain a certain logical contradiction, but probabilistic evidence.
H0 assumed true → distribution of possible outcomes → observed outcome → decision
The case: administrative processing times

A public agency reports a mean application-processing time of 48 days. After a reorganisation, administrators are concerned that processing times have increased.

H0: μ = 48     H1: μ > 48

A sample produces a mean of 50 days. Are two additional days enough to conclude that the process has worsened?

Sampling variability.
Even if the true mean were still 48 days, different samples would produce different means.
Test question: how plausible is it to observe a mean of 50, or an even more extreme value, if H0 were true?

A decision requires a test statistic, a reference distribution, and a decision rule.

Objective: understand the logic of hypothesis testing; select and interpret z, t, and proportion tests; distinguish decision errors; and analyse power.

Type I error
α = P(rejecting H0 | H0 true)

It is a false positive: we report a change that does not exist.

Example: concluding that the process has worsened when the mean time is still 48 days.

Type II error
β = P(not rejecting H0 | H1 true)

It is a false negative: we fail to detect a real change.

Example: failing to detect a genuine increase in processing times.

The alarm metaphor
False alarm
The alarm sounds, but there is no problem. This is a Type I error.
Undetected problem
A problem exists, but the alarm does not sound. This is a Type II error.

What is power?

Power measures the test's ability to detect a difference when that difference truly exists.

Power = 1 − β = P(rejecting H0 | H1 true)
The key figure: two distributions

The blue curve describes what would happen if H0 were true. The orange curve describes a specific alternative H1. The critical value separates the non-rejection region from the rejection region.

H₀H₁αβPower
n increases

The curves become narrower, overlap less, and power increases.

The effect increases

The curves move farther apart: a large difference is easier to detect.

Variability increases

The curves become wider and power decreases.

Power is not a single number for all of H1. It depends on the specific value of μ under the alternative, that is, on the effect size being considered.

The trade-off between α and β

Holding n, σ, and the effect constant, increasing α makes it easier to reject H0 and increases power, but it also increases the risk of a false positive.

How can both be reduced?

In general, more information must be collected: increasing n makes the distributions more concentrated and allows both α and β to be kept under control.

Decisions in Politics, Law and Economics

The same statistical error can have very different economic consequences depending on the context.

Policy-compliance monitoring

H₀: the mean weight meets the standard.
α error: unnecessarily stopping a correctly operating line.
β error: failing to detect packages that do not meet the standard.

Public communication

H₀: the new information campaign does not increase participation.
α error: funding an ineffective campaign.
β error: discarding a genuinely useful campaign.

Public administration

H₀: processing times have not increased.
α error: intervening in a stable process.
β error: ignoring a genuine deterioration.

Labour economics

H₀: the training does not improve productivity.
α error: funding an ineffective programme.
β error: giving up an effective programme.

Statistical significance ≠ substantive relevance

A new system reduces mean handling time from 30.0 to 29.8 minutes. With a very large sample, a difference of 0.2 minutes may be statistically significant.

Policy question

Does a reduction of 12 seconds justify the cost of the new system?

The test measures statistical evidence. A policy or institutional decision must also consider costs, benefits, equity, and effect size.

Two short worked examples

Read the question, identify the required method, and then follow the calculation and interpretation.

Worked example 1

A one-sample test

Question. A sample gives x̄=12.4, hypothesised mean μ₀=12, s=2, and n=100. Compute the test statistic.

Solution. t=(12.4−12)/(2/√100)=0.4/0.2=2. For a two-sided 5% test with many degrees of freedom, this is just beyond the approximate critical value 1.96, so H₀ is rejected.
Worked example 2

Type I error in context

Question. H₀ states that a public programme has no effect. What is a Type I error?

Solution. It is concluding that the programme has an effect when in reality it has none. The significance level α controls the long-run probability of this error under H₀.
Now try one yourself · 4 points
Formulating and concluding a test
A study compares mean case duration before and after a reform. State the null and alternative hypotheses and explain what p=0.018 means at the 5% level.
Show the guided solution

For a two-sided comparison, H0: μ_before=μ_after and H1: μ_before≠μ_after. A p-value of 0.018 means that, if H0 were true and the model assumptions held, a result at least this incompatible with H0 would occur with probability 1.8%. Since 0.018<0.05, reject H0; do not interpret the p-value as the probability that H0 is true.
