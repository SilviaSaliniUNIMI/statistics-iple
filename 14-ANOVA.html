Before ANOVA: comparing two groups

When there are only two groups, an independent-samples t-test is normally used. ANOVA leads to the same conclusion because, with two groups, the F-statistic is the square of the t-statistic.

The independent-samples t-test
H₀: μ₁ = μ₂     H₁: μ₁ ≠ μ₂
t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) / [sp √(1/n₁ + 1/n₂)]
sp² = [(n₁−1)s₁² + (n₂−1)s₂²] / (n₁+n₂−2)

This is the equal-variance version, consistent with classical ANOVA.

The critical value and degrees of freedom
df = (n₁ − 1) + (n₂ − 1) = n₁ + n₂ − 2 = n − 2

Degrees of freedom depend on the number of parameters estimated, not on an arbitrary rule. In the first group, we estimate one mean and retain n₁−1 independent deviations; in the second, we estimate a second mean and retain n₂−1.

Why do we subtract 2? With two independent samples, we estimate two means, one for each group. Therefore, from the total sample size n=n₁+n₂, we subtract two degrees of freedom.

In a one-sample t-test, by contrast, only one mean is estimated, so we use:

df = n − 1

In a two-tailed test at the 5% level, H₀ is rejected when:

There is also a paired-samples t-test. It is used when observations in the two groups are naturally linked, for example before-and-after policy measurements in the same jurisdictions or two assessments of the same courts. In that case, differences within pairs are analysed rather than two independent samples.
Learn more: when variances are unequal

Welch's t-test does not use the pooled variance and calculates approximate degrees of freedom. It is often preferable when the dispersions of the two groups are very different.

Why is it called the Fisher–Snedecor F-distribution? The letter F refers to Ronald A. Fisher, who developed analysis of variance and the comparison of variance estimates in the 1920s. George W. Snedecor helped systematise and disseminate this ratio, using the letter F in Fisher's honour. For this reason, the distribution is often called the Fisher–Snedecor F-distribution.
Why is it skewed? F is a ratio of two non-negative quantities. It can therefore take only values greater than or equal to zero and has a right-hand tail.
p-value: is the probability, under H₀, that the random variable F takes a value at least as large as the observed F-statistic; graphically, it is the area to the right of the observed value. If F exceeds the critical value, the result falls in the rejection region.
F = between-group mean square / within-group mean square

Statistical significance and relevance

The p-value measures the evidence against H₀. Eta-squared, written using the Greek letter eta as η², instead measures the proportion of total variability associated with group membership. It is not H squared.

Eta-squared: η2 = between-group sum of squares / total sum of squares

Before applying ANOVA

Classical ANOVA relies on several conditions. In this e-tivity, we do not formalise them as model assumptions; instead, we use them as preliminary checks to understand when the result requires caution.

1. Independent observations

Each observation must provide new information. The same case, person, or court must not be counted multiple times as though they were independent units.

This is a data-design issue: it cannot be resolved by looking at a graph. We need to know how the observations were collected.
2. Groups without severe anomalies

Especially when groups are small, highly skewed distributions or extreme values can make means and variances unstable.

For this reason, always inspect the data in each group using dot plots, histograms, or boxplots before interpreting the p-value.

There is no need to introduce residuals yet: at this stage, it is enough to check how values are distributed within each group.
3. Dispersions that are not too different

Classical ANOVA works best when the groups have similar variability.

If the dispersions are very different: the result should be interpreted cautiously; Welch's ANOVA may be preferable.
4. Extreme values

A single very distant value can change the group mean, increase its variance, and influence the F-statistic.

Do not remove it automatically: we need to determine whether it is an error, a genuine rare case, or a signal that the groups are not homogeneous.
Caveat: these checks do not replace a formal diagnostic analysis. Later, when ANOVA is presented as a statistical model, the conditions can be formulated more precisely.

From one-way to multi-way ANOVA

One-way ANOVA considers a single factor. In real applications, it may be useful to study several factors simultaneously.

One factor
Jurisdiction → Case-resolution time

We compare mean times across A, B, and C.

Two factors
Jurisdiction + court size → Resolution time

Multi-way ANOVA makes it possible to study multiple factors and their possible interactions.

Parallel lines: no evident interaction
Non-parallel lines: possible interaction
Interaction: the effect of a legal procedure may change depending on court size. In this case, describing the two factors separately is not sufficient.
Looking ahead: in later courses, you will see that ANOVA can also be formulated as a statistical model and is closely connected to regression. Here, we focus on decomposing variability and comparing means.

Two short worked examples

Read the question, identify the required method, and then follow the calculation and interpretation.

Worked example 1

Completing an ANOVA table

Question. There are k=3 groups and n=30 observations. SSB=120 and SSW=270. Calculate the degrees of freedom, mean squares, and F.

Solution. df_between=2, df_within=27. MSB=120/2=60; MSW=270/27=10; F=60/10=6.
Worked example 2

Decision from the F statistic

Question. For the previous example, the 5% critical value is 3.35. What is the conclusion?

Solution. Because 6>3.35, reject the null hypothesis that all population means are equal. ANOVA shows that at least one mean differs, but a post-hoc comparison is needed to identify which groups differ.
Now try one yourself · 4 points
Completing and interpreting ANOVA
An ANOVA compares mean court duration in four jurisdictions using n=120 cases. State the degrees of freedom for between groups, within groups and total, and explain what a large F statistic indicates.
Show the guided solution

With k=4 and n=120: df_between=k−1=3, df_within=n−k=116, and df_total=n−1=119. A large F means that variability among jurisdiction means is large relative to variability within jurisdictions; the p-value determines whether this is statistically significant.
