Section 2

From regions to the national mean

Four regions have different population sizes and average household incomes. The national mean must weight each regional mean by the number of households in that region.

Average household income
RegionHouseholds \(n_h\)Mean \(\bar x_h\)Weight \(n_h/n\)Contribution
Total—Overall mean—
\[\bar x=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\frac{n_h}{n}\,\bar x_h\]
Common mistake. The simple mean of the four regional means would give Bari and Milan the same weight, even though their populations differ greatly.

The sum of all observations can be rewritten as the sum of the group totals:

\[\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{i=1}^{n_h}x_{hi}=\sum_{h=1}^{H}n_h\bar x_h\]

Dividing by \(n=\sum_h n_h\) gives the weighted mean. Each region contributes in proportion to the number of households it contains.

Section 3

Two sources of variability

Observations may differ because they are far from their own group mean and because the group means are far from the overall mean.

Within-group variability
Differences among observations belonging to the same group.
Between-group variability
Differences between group means and the overall mean.
Total variability
Sum of the two contributions.
\[V_T=V_W+V_B\]
\[V_W=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\frac{n_h}{n}\,V_h\]
\[V_B=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\frac{n_h}{n}(\bar x_h-\bar x)^2\]

Convention used in the e-tivity. We calculate descriptive population variances by dividing by \(n\). In inferential statistics, sums of squares and degrees of freedom require different denominators.

Four extreme cases

Limiting cases help clarify what each component measures.

Interpretation key. Grouping is informative when a substantial share of total variability is between groups. If the between component is close to zero, knowing the group does not help much in explaining the observed differences.
Section 6

How a deviation is decomposed

Every deviation from the overall mean can be separated into a within-group part and a part due to the position of the group.

\[x_{hi}-\bar x=(x_{hi}-\bar x_h)+(\bar x_h-\bar x)\]

Graphical decomposition of a deviation

The blue arrow leads from the observation to its group mean; the orange arrow leads from the group mean to the overall mean. The oriented sum of the two arrows equals the total deviation.

We square and sum over all observations:

\[\sum_h\sum_i(x_{hi}-\bar x)^2=\sum_h\sum_i(x_{hi}-\bar x_h)^2+\sum_h n_h(\bar x_h-\bar x)^2+2\sum_h(\bar x_h-\bar x)\sum_i(x_{hi}-\bar x_h)\]

The cross-product term is zero because, within each group, the sum of deviations from the group mean is zero:

\[\sum_i(x_{hi}-\bar x_h)=0\]

Dividing everything by \(n\), we obtain \(V_T=V_W+V_B\).

Mean

\[E(a+bX)=a+bE(X)\]

The mean is linear: shifts and changes of scale pass directly through the operator.

Variance

\[V(X+a)=V(X),\qquad V(bX)=b^2V(X)\]

Variance is not linear. A shift does not change it; multiplication scales it by the square.

Section 7

Applications: where do the differences come from?

The theorem transforms an overall measure into a substantive question about the structure of the data.

🏛️ Politics
Does voter turnout differ because regions have different average levels, or because constituencies vary greatly within each region?
⚖️ Law
Are differences in court duration due to differences between jurisdictions, or to variability within each jurisdiction?
🏥 Healthcare
Do outcomes differ between hospitals, or is there great heterogeneity among patients within the same hospital?
🎓 Education
Are there differences between schools, or among students within the same school?
💶 Labour economics
Do pay levels differ between departments, or is there high dispersion within departments?
🌍 Economics
Differences between regions or sectors, and variability within each subpopulation.
A bridge to later tools

The decomposition of variance underlies analysis of variance (ANOVA), regression, and hierarchical models. In this e-tivity, we do not perform inference: we learn to interpret the two sources of variability descriptively.

Preparing for the exam

This final section contains two short worked examples followed by a 4-point practice question in the style of the IPLE written examination.

Two short worked examples

Read the question, identify the required method, and then follow the calculation and interpretation.

Worked example 1

Decomposing total variability

Question. For three regions, total sum of squares is 500 and within-group sum of squares is 380. Find the between-group sum of squares.

Solution. SS_between = SS_total − SS_within = 500 − 380 = 120. This is the part of variability associated with differences among regional means.
Worked example 2

Reading the variance ratio

Question. Two classifications have the same total variability. Classification A leaves little variability within groups; classification B leaves a great deal. Which better separates the groups?

Solution. Classification A. When within-group variation is small relative to between-group variation, group membership explains more of the observed differences.
Now try one yourself · 4 points
Within- and between-group variation
Two regions have the same national mean income, but very different regional means. Explain how total variability can be decomposed and what a large between-region component means.
Show the guided solution

Total variability is the sum of within-region variability and between-region variability. A large between component means that regional means are far from the national mean and that region membership explains a substantial share of overall income differences; it does not by itself establish a causal regional effect.
