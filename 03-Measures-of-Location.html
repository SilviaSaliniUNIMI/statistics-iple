Measures of Location

Mode, median, mean, quartiles, and percentiles: how they are calculated, what they tell us, and when they can mislead.

Section 1

Start with the mode: what occurs most often?

Measures of location answer different questions. We begin with the mode, the most frequent value or category; then move to the median, which identifies the middle position; and finally to the mean, which redistributes the total equally.

All three choices may be meaningful, but they describe different aspects. In this dataset, the mean is 22.3, the median is 22.5, and the modes are 20 and 24. The rest of the e-tivity will show why these values may diverge.
Mean
It is the value that would result from redistributing the total equally. It uses all observations.
Median
It divides the ordered observations into two groups of equal size.
Mode
It is the value, category, or region of the distribution that occurs most frequently.
Guiding idea. A summary measure simplifies reality. To interpret it correctly, you need to know how it is constructed and which information it may lose.
Section 2

How the mean is formed: redistribution and balance

The arithmetic mean is not merely a formula. It can be interpreted as the value obtained by redistributing the total equally or as the balance point of the distribution.

\[\bar{x}=\frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n}\]

The mean as a balance point

Redistribution.
The sum of the values remains the same, but it is distributed equally among all units.
Balance.
The sum of deviations from the mean is zero: negative and positive deviations cancel each other out.
\[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-\bar{x})=0\]
Because it directly uses every value, the mean changes when even a single observation changes, especially when the change is very large.

How the median is formed: ordering and cumulative frequencies

The median is a positional measure. To find it, knowing the values is not enough: they must be ordered and the point dividing the distribution into two halves must be identified.

1. First, order the data

The following data are the same as in the first section, but in random order.

The median cannot be identified without an ordering relation.
2. Find the central position

With n = 10, the two central positions are the fifth and sixth. The median is their mean:

\[Me=\frac{x_{(5)}+x_{(6)}}{2}=\frac{22+23}{2}=22.5\]
If the number of observations is odd, the median is the single central value. If it is even, it lies between the two central values.
The same idea through cumulative frequencies
ValueFrequencyCumulative frequencyCumulative proportion
How to read it. The cumulative frequency indicates how many observations are less than or equal to a given value. The 50% point is reached between 22 and 23; therefore, the median is 22.5.
Key concept. The median requires an ordering relation. It is therefore defined for quantitative and ordinal qualitative variables, but not for nominal variables such as political party or type of legal proceeding.

When we do not know the individual values, but only the frequencies within intervals, we can identify the median class. To estimate a value within the interval, an assumption about the distribution of the observations is required.

Identify the median interval

The total is n = 40. The central position is n/2 = 20.

The cumulative frequency rises from 8 to 23 in the interval 12–24 hours: this is the median interval.

The median is not automatically the midpoint of the interval, namely 18. Without the individual data, we do not know exactly where the 15 observations in the interval are located.

Intuitive interpolation within the median interval

\[Me \approx L+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-N_{prec}}{n_m}\,a\]
In our example: lower boundary of the median interval \(L=12\), preceding cumulative frequency \(N_{prev}=8\), interval frequency \(n_m=15\), width \(a=12\). Linear interpolation gives \(Me\approx21.6\) hours.
Interpolation is an estimate: it assumes that observations are uniformly distributed within the interval.
Section 5

The mode: the most frequent value, category, or interval

The mode is the value or category with the largest frequency. It can be used for quantitative, ordinal, and nominal variables. With grouped continuous data, the modal interval must be identified from frequency density when class widths differ.

How to identify and interpret the mode
1 · Compare frequencies
Find the value or category with the largest absolute or relative frequency.
2 · Check for ties
One maximum gives a unimodal distribution; two equal maxima give a bimodal distribution; several maxima give a multimodal distribution.
3 · Interpret in context
The mode identifies what is most common. It does not necessarily identify the middle of the distribution.
Important. A distribution can have no unique mode, and two distributions can have the same mode but very different medians and overall shapes.

Quartiles and percentiles: dividing an ordered distribution

Quantiles identify relative positions. Quartiles divide the distribution into four parts; percentiles into one hundred parts. They do not indicate a proportion of the maximum value, but the percentage of observations below a given threshold.

Section 8

The robustness of the median

A measure is robust when it does not change drastically in the presence of a few anomalous or extreme observations. The median is much more robust than the mean.

DatasetMeanMedianEffect of the extreme value
An upward extreme value
A downward extreme value
How much must the sample change to shift the median?

Changing a single extreme value does not shift the central position. To change the median substantially, a considerable proportion of the central observations must be altered.

The median has a breakdown point close to 50%: it remains resistant as long as fewer than half of the data are arbitrarily contaminated.

This property underlies the use of the median for income, property prices, waiting times, and other distributions with tails or extreme values.

Section 9

Mean and median as an indication of skewness

The relative positions of the mean and median provide an initial indication of the shape of the distribution. This is a useful descriptive rule, not conclusive evidence of symmetry or skewness.

Symmetry
The mean and median tend to coincide or be very close.
Right tail
High values pull the mean to the right: often mean > median.
Left tail
Low values pull the mean to the left: often mean < median.
Caution. Similar mean and median values do not guarantee that the distribution is symmetric. A bimodal or irregular distribution may have the same two measures.

Section 13

Final quiz

Check whether you can distinguish the meaning, calculation, and appropriate use of the main measures of location.

Key messages
  • The mean is a balance point and uses all values.
  • The median requires ordering and divides the distribution into two halves.
  • For intervals of unequal width, the modal interval is the one with the highest density.
  • Quartiles and percentiles describe relative positions, not percentages of the maximum value.
  • The median is robust, but the mean may be more informative in symmetric distributions without important outliers.
  • The difference between the mean and median is an indication, not a complete diagnosis, of skewness.

Preparing for the exam

This final section contains two short worked examples followed by a 4-point practice question in the style of the IPLE written examination.

Two short worked examples

Read the question, identify the required method, and then follow the calculation and interpretation.

Worked example 1

Mean or median?

Question. Five monthly legal-aid waiting times are 8, 9, 10, 11, and 42 days. Calculate the mean and median and choose the better typical value.

Solution. Mean = 80/5 = 16 days; median = 10 days. The extreme value 42 pulls the mean upwards, so the median is the more representative centre for this small, skewed set.
Worked example 2

A weighted mean

Question. A policy course has 20 students with an average of 24 and 30 students with an average of 27. Find the overall average.

Solution. Use group sizes as weights: (20×24 + 30×27)/50 = 25.8. Averaging 24 and 27 directly would be wrong because the groups have different sizes.
