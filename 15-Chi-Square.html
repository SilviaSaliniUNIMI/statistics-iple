Independence and the product rule

Under independence, the joint probability is the product of the marginal probabilities.

From probability to expected frequency
P(Aᵢ ∩ Bⱼ)=P(Aᵢ)P(Bⱼ)

The groups do not have the same size: 260 citizens received the social-media information channel, 210 received the email information channel, and 130 received no information channel. Outcome totals also differ.

P(Social)=260/600=0,433
P(High participation)=260/600=0,433
P(Social ∩ High participation)=0,433×0,433=0,188
E=600×0.188=112.67

Observed frequencies, expected frequencies, and contingencies

We call the difference between the observed frequency and the expected frequency under independence a contingency.

Eᵢⱼ=(row total i × column total j)/n
Contingency cᵢⱼ=Oᵢⱼ−Eᵢⱼ

The contingencies balance out. Within every row and column, the O−E differences sum to zero; to construct an overall measure, we cannot simply add them.

Actually calculating chi-square

Each contingency is squared and divided by the cell's expected frequency. Chi-square is the sum of the nine contributions.

χ²=ΣᵢΣⱼ (Oᵢⱼ−Eᵢⱼ)²/Eᵢⱼ
One contribution, step by step

For the Social media × High participation cell:

O=90
E=(260×260)/600=112.67
O−E=23.33
(O−E)²/E=(23.33²)/66.67=8.17

Squaring makes both positive and negative deviations contribute positively.

Why four? Once the margins are fixed, we can choose four cells freely; the other five are determined by the row and column totals.

From the observed statistic to the p-value

Under H₀, the statistic approximately follows a chi-square distribution with four degrees of freedom.

The distribution is right-skewed and takes only non-negative values.
The p-value is the area to the right of the observed value.

Which cells explain the association?

After the global test, we examine Pearson residuals: contingencies expressed on the scale of the expected frequency.

Pearson residual=(Oᵢⱼ−Eᵢⱼ)/√Eᵢⱼ
Residuals cell by cell
How to interpret them
Positive: more cases than expected under independence.
Negative: fewer cases than expected.
Close to zero: a cell that is broadly compatible with independence.

Significance and strength of association

The p-value also depends on sample size; Cramér's V instead describes the relative strength of association.

V=√[χ²/(n×min(r−1,c−1))]

The percentages remain identical: only the counts change.

Total n600
χ²47,89
p-value<0,001
Cramér's V0.200
χ² increases with n V remains constant
Conclusion: an unchanged association becomes more easily significant in a large sample. Therefore, the p-value and the strength of association answer different questions.
Conditions for application

Independent observations, mutually exclusive categories, and expected frequencies that are not too small.

2 × 2 tables with small counts

Fisher's exact test may be preferable.

Connection with Simpson: the test describes the table being analysed. A third variable may still modify or reverse the marginal relationship.

Two short worked examples

Read the question, identify the required method, and then follow the calculation and interpretation.

Worked example 1

Expected frequencies

Question. In a 2×2 table, each row total is 100 and the column totals are 90 and 110. What are the expected frequencies under independence?

Solution. For each row, E₁=100×90/200=45 and E₂=100×110/200=55. The expected table is therefore (45,55) in both rows.
Worked example 2

Chi-square contribution

Question. Observed rows are (60,40) and (30,70), with expected rows (45,55). Calculate χ².

Solution. χ²=Σ(O−E)²/E = 225/45 + 225/55 + 225/45 + 225/55 ≈18.18. With df=(2−1)(2−1)=1, this is strong evidence against independence.
Now try one yourself · 4 points
A chi-square test of independence
In a 2×3 table of education level by voting status, explain how to calculate an expected frequency and how to interpret a large positive Pearson residual.
Show the guided solution

Under independence, E_ij=(row total×column total)/n. A large positive Pearson residual (O−E)/√E means that the observed cell count is substantially higher than expected under independence and that the cell contributes positively and strongly to the chi-square statistic.
